# Explainability Cognitive Governance Checklist

Use this checklist before adding or editing migrated explainability content.

## Core Rule

Every chart should leave the user with one dominant takeaway. Supporting text should reinforce that takeaway, not introduce competing interpretations.

## Beginner Flow

- Explain what is happening before explaining the visualization.
- Keep Simple Mode short enough to understand in seconds.
- Use one consequence, one guardrail, and one next step.
- Prefer practical wording over metric or chart terminology.
- Do not require users to remember earlier sections to understand the current one.

## Language

- Avoid chart-first phrases such as "this chart shows" or "the graph indicates".
- Avoid analyst language such as "multivariate exploration", "correlation-driven inference", or "system-level analytical pathway".
- Avoid dramatic or certain claims when evidence is weak.
- Prefer measured words such as "appears", "suggests", "likely", and "may indicate".

## Glossary

- Replace technical terms with plain language when possible.
- Keep glossary support secondary and contextual.
- Do not use the glossary to compensate for unclear primary explanation.

## Continuation

- Recommend one follow-up path before broader investigation.
- Explain why the next chart matters in one short reason.
- Allow shallow completion when the current state is mild, weak, or already clear.
- Do not pressure users into long investigation chains.

## Optional Detail

- Keep analyst detail and visualization anatomy optional.
- Do not let optional learning become the main reading path.
- Keep guided reading concise and focused on safe interpretation.

## Weak Data

- Prefer "there is not enough evidence to conclude" over forced interpretation.
- Do not inflate weak, sparse, noisy, or unstable patterns into strong insights.
- Raise uncertainty once clearly, then move forward.
